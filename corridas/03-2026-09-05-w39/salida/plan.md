### `blog-01` · Blog · 2026-09-22

**Stop Arguing Coverage. Start Arguing Markets.**

You walk into the budget meeting with the slide that says you're live in a dozen-plus languages. The CFO's first question is the one that slide can't answer: which of those markets is actually working?

That's where the coverage argument runs out of road. How many languages a program reaches is a statement about the size of the operation. It says nothing about whether the operation is producing anything finance would call a return. Coverage answers "how big." Finance is asking "so what."

The fix isn't a bigger coverage number. It's a different kind of number, measured in the vocabulary finance already speaks: demand, competitive position, customer behavior, market by market. That is exactly the language InRegion already speaks: "campaigns should match local demand, local rivals, and local buyer needs." Trends, competitors, and customer needs aren't localization concepts - they're the three questions a CFO asks about any investment. The argument that survives the meeting isn't "we're live in fourteen markets." It's "here's what happened in this market when we showed up in it correctly."

Case by case, that argument already has evidence behind it. Northvale launched Spanish- and Polish-language sites in 21 days - a speed-to-market result, not a coverage count. Localized content drove a 147% increase in conversions. SEO-built content lifted conversions in Brazil by 63%. Railvia posted 44% compound annual revenue growth over four years. None of these get averaged into one "return on multilingual content" figure - there isn't one, and a program-wide ROI number would sound precise while proving nothing. Each stands on its own market, its own result.

That's four data points, all from Acme's own customers, which is exactly why a CFO deciding next year's budget shouldn't have to take our word alone. It's also exactly what the 2026 Native Reach State of the Industry report exists for: evidence of what market-aligned content returns across the industry, not just across our customers.

The next budget conversation doesn't need a bigger coverage slide. It needs a market-by-market case, backed by evidence wider than any single company's customer list.
